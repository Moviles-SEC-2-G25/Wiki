# Interview Transcript (English) — Sebastián Maldonado interviewing Tomás

**Maldo:** Hi Tomás, thanks for making time for this. This is for a project for the Mobile Application Construction class. I'm going to ask you a few questions about your experience with lunch and the cafeterias on campus. Could you give us a quick introduction of yourself, and confirm you're okay with me recording this conversation for academic use in the course?

**Tomás:** Hi Sebastián, thanks for the invitation. As you already said, I'm Tomás, a senior-year Chemical Engineering student. I have quite a bit of experience with the central cafeteria because I was around both before and after the renovation, and I used to eat lunch there very frequently.

**Maldo:** Perfect, are you okay with the recording?

**Tomás:** Yes, agreed. Let's start.

**Maldo:** How many times a week do you eat lunch at a cafeteria on campus?

**Tomás:** At the central cafeteria specifically, I used to eat lunch there at least three times a week — sometimes four, depending on the schedule for that particular semester or week.

**Maldo:** Got it. Walk me through the last time you had lunch on campus, step by step, from when you left your previous class until you sat back down to eat or study.

**Tomás:** Well, I would leave class around 11:20, and what I was really thinking about was having lunch. However, the central cafeteria doesn't open until 11:30, so I had to wait. And even though I'd arrive relatively early, the line that formed was really long and took up a lot of my time. That's more or less the experience I had, from leaving class until I got to the cafeteria or restaurant.

**Maldo:** Got it. How much actual free time do you have between that class and the next one?

**Tomás:** Ugh, between an hour and twenty minutes and an hour and a half at most is what I have available for lunch between classes around midday.

**Maldo:** I understand. And how much of that time normally goes into waiting in line?

**Tomás:** Like a good 25, 30 minutes, depending also on the time of day — but that's roughly the average.

**Maldo:** Right, and when you're in line, what frustrates or worries you the most?

**Tomás:** What frustrates me the most is that I could be resting, making the most of that time, but instead I have to stand there in line to order my lunch, and then I have to wait in another line to pick it up. It's really frustrating to see time wasted like that, knowing that during that time slot I'd like to be de-stressing, thinking about other things — not standing there doing nothing.

**Maldo:** Right, and have you ever decided not to eat lunch, or to eat something different, because you didn't have time to wait in line?

**Tomás:** Yes, many times — either it gets too late, or the line is so long that I'd rather eat a sandwich outside, from Ml or somewhere like that. Even if it doesn't fill me up, or even if it's not the best lunch, as long as I avoid that line — that's what normally happens when I decide not to eat at the cafeteria.

**Maldo:** Got it. I want to share an idea with you: an app where you order your lunch in advance from your phone and pick it up already prepared, without waiting in line. What would you think about that? Be as complete and specific as you can.

**Tomás:** No, honestly, that would be great, because thinking about it — I'd just leave class, or even a few minutes before, put together my order in the app, and I'd only have to show up to pick it up, skipping the line entirely. That would be the best-case scenario — I'd have lunch and still have time to rest, relax, or do other things. That would be an amazing idea, and honestly, I'd support it.

**Maldo:** Understood. And what would make you doubt or distrust using something like that?

**Tomás:** There would really be two things. The first is payment — because you never really know if the payment actually went through, or if the order was actually placed. Those are the things that would worry me the most about the app. Other than that, I guess making sure the order is ready on time, so it's not a situation where I order, arrive, and it's not there, or nobody knows what happened with the order — that would matter to me the most.

**Maldo:** Right, and what would that app need to have for you to use it every day you decide to eat at the cafeteria, not just once?

**Tomás:** I think the basics of a good app: good performance, a friendly interface that doesn't lag, and different payment methods in general. That's what I'd look for in an app to use frequently.

**Maldo:** And would you like to have the option to pay from within the app?

**Tomás:** Yes, of course. Something that would be really cool is having a saved-cards system, or even PSE — either of those would be great. I wouldn't have a problem paying in the app as long as it's guaranteed to be secure.

**Maldo:** Right, is there anything else about your experience eating lunch on campus that you think I should know, that I didn't ask about?

**Tomás:** Yes, well — I really feel that at the central cafeteria the food itself is pretty good, but the lines are what frustrate people. And it's not just the line to order — it's also the line to pick it up. That's something that, if solved, would also be great to take into account, because if it's 20-25 minutes ordering lunch, plus another 10 waiting for it, you've already added up 30-40 minutes just ordering and waiting for lunch. So if the app manages to solve both of those wait times, that would be incredible.

**Maldo:** And regarding the app, is there anything else that raises doubts or expectations for you? If not, no problem.

**Tomás:** No, I think I've already covered everything about the app. Maybe — it shouldn't just be for the central cafeteria, but for more spots or restaurants within the university. That would also be really cool, because I understand not everyone likes the central cafeteria, and the university has several restaurants that would also be worth implementing in this app.

**Maldo:** Thank you so much for your time, this really helps with the project.

**Tomás:** Alright, thanks.
